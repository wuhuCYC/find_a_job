## 2. OPT and STEM OPT

### 2.1 Current rules **[IN EFFECT - background unless noted]**
Sources: 8 CFR 214.2(f) [18]; USCIS Policy Manual Vol. 2, Part F, Ch. 5 [17]; USCIS STEM OPT page [19]; DHS Study in the States STEM OPT Hub [20].

**Post-completion OPT (12 months)**
- You get up to 12 months of OPT per degree level. The work must be directly related to your major. For post-completion OPT, a job counts as employment only if it is at least 20 hours per week.
- **Filing window:** you can file Form I-765 from **90 days before** your program end date until **60 days after** it. You must also file **within 30 days** of the date your DSO enters the OPT recommendation in SEVIS. You request a start date that falls within the 60-day period after your program ends.
- **Fee:** $470 on paper or $410 online, as stated in Sep 2026 coverage of the OPT fee proposal [59][60]. Premium processing is available for OPT I-765 applications (background).
- **Unemployment limit:** at most **90 days** in total during post-completion OPT. The STEM extension adds **60 days**, for **150 days in total** across OPT and STEM OPT. Days are counted as calendar days while your EAD is valid and you have no qualifying job.
- **Reporting:** report your employer and any change **within 10 days**, through the SEVP Portal or your DSO. If a job is not in your SEVIS record, it counts as unemployment.

**STEM OPT extension (24 months)**
- **Degree:** it must be on DHS's STEM Designated Degree Program List. EECS, CS, and EE CIP codes are normally on the list, but confirm the CIP code printed on your I-20.
- **Employer requirements:**
  - The employer must be **enrolled in E-Verify** and in good standing.
  - You and the employer must sign a **Form I-983 training plan**, which goes to your DSO before you file.
  - The job must be paid and at least 20 hours per week, with pay comparable to similarly situated U.S. workers.
  - There must be a real employer-employee relationship. Unpaid or volunteer work does not qualify, and the employer that signs the I-983 must provide the training.
  - There are self-evaluations at 12 months and at the end, plus validation reports every 6 months. The employer must report your departure within 5 business days. DHS may visit the worksite.
- **Timing:** file up to **90 days before** your OPT EAD expires, and within 60 days of the DSO's recommendation. If you file on time, your work authorization is **automatically extended for up to 180 days** while the application is pending.
- **Lifetime limit:** at most two STEM extensions, and the second one must be based on a higher degree.

**Cap-gap**
- If a cap-subject H-1B petition requesting a change of status is filed on time, your F-1 status and OPT work authorization carry over until **April 1** of the fiscal year. Before the H-1B modernization rule took effect on Jan 17, 2025, the cutoff was Oct 1 [16]. The April 1 date is background knowledge; confirm it on the USCIS cap-gap page.

### 2.3 Practical risk level **[ASSESSMENT]**
- **Next 6 months:** OPT and STEM OPT keep running under the current rules. The most likely near-term change is **higher cost** (proposal B), not elimination.
- **Restriction or elimination (item D):** this needs a published proposal, a comment period, and a final rule, and the D/S rule shows courts may block it. A final restriction before mid or late 2027 seems unlikely. It is unknown whether such a rule would reach people **already** holding OPT or STEM OPT, because no text exists yet. Your own STEM extension, which you would probably file in 2027, carries a **moderate, unquantifiable** risk from new fees or conditions.
- **If the D/S rule comes back:** the main effects are more paperwork, possible delays to start dates, and a shorter grace period, not loss of OPT.
- **What to do about it:** file OPT early in your window, keep your status clean, keep your SEVIS record accurate, and prefer employers that meet STEM OPT requirements from the first day.

## 3. H-1B changes since 2025

### 3.1 The $100,000 H-1B proclamation (signed Sep 19, 2025)

**Who it applies to** (USCIS guidance of Oct 20, 2025, as summarized by BakerHostetler, Ogletree, UMich, and Yale) [1][2][22][23][24][25][26]:
- **Applies to:** new H-1B petitions filed **on or after 12:01 a.m. EDT, Sep 21, 2025** for a worker **outside the U.S.** who has no valid H-1B visa. It also applies to petitions that ask for consular notification, port-of-entry inspection, or pre-flight inspection. If USCIS denies a change of status or extension and approves the petition only for consular notification, the payment also applies [31].
- **Does not apply to:** petitions filed before Sep 21, 2025; H-1B visas that are already valid; and **change of status (for example F-1 to H-1B), amendments, or extensions for people inside the U.S., as long as USCIS approves them.** This is why most F-1 graduates were not affected.
- **Exceptions:** the DHS Secretary could grant national-interest exceptions, but only in extraordinarily rare circumstances [1][22].
- **Actual use:** employers paid the $100,000 for only about **700 people** in the first year, according to the Sep 2026 proclamation as reported by Forbes [39].

**Status now:**
- The proclamation is legally in effect through Sep 21, 2027, **but the payment is not being collected**, because the implementing policy remains vacated.
- DiRaimondo & Schroeder and Fragomen read the vacatur as also covering the extension, since it is framed as an extension. They note this is **subject to judicial clarification** [37][38].
- The fee could come back if the First Circuit reverses on the merits or the Supreme Court steps in. **No Supreme Court stay application was found** in this research.

**What it means for you:**
- An F-1 to H-1B change of status filed while you are in the U.S. was never subject to the fee.
- It could matter only if you end up needing consular processing **and** the fee is reinstated. That could happen if you travel abroad while your change of status is pending (which abandons it), if the change of status is denied, or if you are outside the U.S. when the petition is filed.

### 3.2 Proposed $103,265 fee on every cap-subject H-1B petition **[PROPOSED]**
- **Rule:** "Fee for Certain H-1B Petitions," published **Aug 25, 2026** (FR Doc. 2026-17324; Docket USCIS-2026-0298). Comments are due **Sep 24, 2026** [9][10].
- **Amount:** **$103,265 per cap-subject petition**, paid at filing on top of all other fees [10].
- **Covered petitions:** the **regular cap and the advanced-degree (master's) cap**, whether the petition asks for change of status or consular notification. Unlike the proclamation, **F-1 to H-1B cap cases would be covered** [10][50][51].
- **Not covered:** cap-exempt employers (universities, affiliated nonprofits, nonprofit and governmental research organizations); extensions, changes of employer, and other petitions for workers already counted against the cap; and H-1B1 petitions [10][50].
- **Revenue:** DHS projects about **$8.8 billion a year**, based on 85,000 petitions. The money would be split across USCIS (34.2%), EOIR (33.7%), DOL (13.8%), ICE (11.9%), DOS (5.5%), and CBP (0.9%) [10][50].
- **Status:** not in effect. DHS must consider the comments and publish a final rule, and court challenges are expected [50][51].
- **[ASSESSMENT] Timing:** if the rule is finalized before **Apr 1, 2027**, it would hit the FY2028 filing season, which is probably your first realistic lottery. Even if it is still pending in Feb-Mar 2027, employers deciding whether to register you will price in the risk. For this search, this is the **single most important open question**.

### 3.3 Wage-weighted H-1B lottery **[IN EFFECT]**
- **Rule:** "Weighted Selection Process for Registrants and Petitioners Seeking To File Cap-Subject H-1B Petitions," 90 FR 60864, published **Dec 29, 2025**, effective **Feb 27, 2026**. It was **used for the FY2027 registration** (Mar 4-19, 2026), the first year without a purely random draw [4][5][6][44][45].
- **How it works:**
  - Each registration is weighted by the highest Department of Labor wage level (I-IV) that the offered salary meets for the job's occupation code (SOC) and work location.
  - My understanding is **Level IV gets 4 entries, Level III 3, Level II 2, and Level I 1**. The snippets I found only said "proportionally greater odds," so confirm this in [4].
  - The weighting applies to both the regular-cap draw and the master's-cap draw. As before, the regular draw runs first, so U.S. master's holders effectively get two chances (background).
- **Details to verify** **[background]**:
  - If the job has several worksites, the **lowest** wage level applies.
  - The later petition must be consistent with the wage level claimed at registration.
  - Generally only guaranteed pay, not discretionary bonus, counts toward the required wage.
- **DHS's own projections** in its Sep 2025 proposal, for a pool about the size of FY2026, were roughly **Level I 15%, Level II 31%, Level III 46%, Level IV 61%**, versus about 30% under a random lottery. **[background: recalled from the proposal's analysis; not re-verified here]**
- **FY2027 actual results by wage level:** **not published or not found.** Overall odds probably improved, because registrations fell about 38% (Section 3.5) **[ASSESSMENT]**.
- **What it means for a new grad [ASSESSMENT]:**
  - A **Level I** offer gets one weight unit; a **Level II** offer gets two, so it has roughly **twice** the odds of Level I.
  - Your wage level depends on:
    - the occupation code the employer uses: for example 15-1252 Software Developers, 15-2051 Data Scientists, 15-2031 Operations Research Analysts, or 13-2051 Financial and Investment Analysts;
    - the metro area: Irvine is in the Anaheim-Santa Ana-Irvine area, and SF, San Jose, and LA are separate areas with different wage levels.
  - Look up all four levels for the relevant code and area on DOL's FLAG wage search [74], and compare them with the **base salary**.
  - Quant trading and hedge-fund new-grad salaries usually clear Level III or IV. Big-tech Bay Area new-grad offers often reach Level II or higher. Smaller fintechs and startups may be at Level I.

### 3.4 DOL prevailing-wage increase **[PROPOSED]**
- DOL announced the proposed rule **Mar 26, 2026**, published it **Mar 27, 2026**, and took comments for 60 days (closed about late May 2026). It covers H-1B, H-1B1, E-3, and PERM [11][52][54].
- **Proposed change:** Level I moves from about the 17th percentile to the **34th**, Level IV from about the 67th to the **88th**, and Levels II and III are set between them. Entry-level requirements would rise by **more than 30%**, and Forbes cites increases of **21-33% by level**. DOL estimates the average certified wage would rise by about **$14,000 a year** [52][53][55].
- **Scope:** would apply to LCAs and prevailing-wage requests filed on or after the effective date. Existing approvals would not be affected [52].
- **Status:** **no final rule found** as of Sep 2026 [54]. Forbes (May 2026) expects the administration to finalize it [55].
- **[ASSESSMENT]:** combined with the weighted lottery, a salary that is Level II today could become Level I under the new levels, and pay below the new Level I could not support an H-1B at all. That pushes employers to sponsor only higher-paid roles.

### 3.5 FY2027 registration results (the March 2026 lottery)
- Registration ran **Mar 4-19, 2026** [6]. By about **Apr 1, 2026**, USCIS had announced that the initial selection was complete and had reached both the regular cap and the master's cap [7][49]. Petitions could be filed **Apr 1 - Jun 30, 2026** [49].
- USCIS announced on **Jul 17, 2026** that the **cap was reached**, so there was **no second selection round** [46][47][48].
- **Volume:** about **211,600 unique-beneficiary registrations**, compared with about 336K-344K for FY2026, a drop of about **38%** **[REPORTED in law-firm summaries; verify on the USCIS registration data page [8]]**.
- **Number selected and selection rates by wage level:** **not located.**
- **Context:** the White House says registrations from the largest IT outsourcing firms fell 92% [3][40].

### 3.6 Other H-1B actions to know about
- **Executive order of Sep 18, 2026 (signed; implementation pending):**
  - Directs DOL, DHS, and State to consider whether an employer had layoffs **in the prior year**, or plans layoffs, affecting similarly situated U.S. workers.
  - Applies to LCAs, petitions, visas, and entry, and reaches beyond the old 90-day, H-1B-dependent-employer rules.
  - DOL must start reviewing previously filed LCA data **within 30 days** [41][42][43].
- **"Reforming the H-1B Nonimmigrant Visa Classification Program" [PRE-PROPOSAL]:** sent to OIRA on **Aug 24, 2026**. It would revise **who qualifies for cap exemption** and increase oversight of **third-party placements** [75]. H-1B reforms were also on the Spring 2026 regulatory agenda [57].
- **Rescinding work permits for H-4 spouses [PRE-PROPOSAL]:** added to the DHS regulatory agenda [70]. Relevant later if you have a spouse on H-4.
- **Consulate backlogs:** H-1B visa appointments have long waits in 2026 [71]. This matters once you hold H-1B status and need a visa stamp to travel.

## 4. Alternatives to the cap-subject H-1B

### 4.1 Cap-exempt H-1B **[IN EFFECT - background, except as noted]**
- **Who is cap-exempt:**
  - institutions of higher education, such as **UC Irvine and every UC campus**, CSU, and private universities;
  - nonprofits related to or affiliated with a university;
  - nonprofit research organizations;
  - governmental research organizations.
- **2025 broadening:** the H-1B modernization rule (effective Jan 17, 2025) widened the research-organization definition to cover organizations where research is a "fundamental activity." It also covers some workers employed by a third party if they spend at least half their time on duties that directly further the qualifying organization's purpose. Confirm the exact wording.
- **Advantages:**
  - No lottery; the employer can file at any time of year.
  - **Exempt from the proposed $103,265 fee** [10][50].
- **Disadvantages:**
  - Academic pay scales are lower.
  - Moving later to a cap-subject employer usually means entering the lottery, unless you were already counted against the cap.
  - Part-time work for a cap-subject employer is allowed only alongside the cap-exempt job.
  - The pending H-1B reform proposal (Section 3.6) may **narrow cap-exemption eligibility** [75] **[PRE-PROPOSAL]**.
- **Relevant options for you:**
  - research software engineer, research programmer, or data roles in UCI and other UC labs;
  - UC Health and UC-affiliated institutes;
  - nonprofit research institutes;
  - some national labs (whether they are cap-exempt depends on how they are structured; ask HR).
  - A good bridge while you keep trying the lottery or build an O-1 record.

### 4.2 O-1A (extraordinary ability) **[IN EFFECT - background]**
- **Eligibility:** evidence meeting at least **3 of 8 criteria**:
  - nationally or internationally recognized awards;
  - memberships that require outstanding achievement;
  - published material about you;
  - judging others' work (peer review counts);
  - original contributions of major significance;
  - scholarly articles;
  - a critical role at a distinguished organization;
  - high salary.
- **Process:** an employer or agent files the petition. There is **no cap and no lottery**, and the first approval can last up to 3 years. The H-1B proclamation and the proposed H-1B fee do **not** apply to O-1, because both are H-1B-specific.
- **[ASSESSMENT]:** realistic for new grads with an unusually strong record, such as several peer-reviewed papers plus reviewing, olympiad or ICPC-level awards, or pay well above market. It is a stretch for a typical new grad. Some high-paying quant and tech employers do use it for standout hires, so ask.

### 4.3 L-1 through a multinational **[IN EFFECT - background]**
- **Requirement:** **one continuous year** of full-time work abroad for a related company (parent, branch, subsidiary, or affiliate) within the 3 years before the petition. L-1B covers specialized knowledge; L-1A covers managers and executives.
- **Strategy:** join a firm with offices abroad, such as London, Hong Kong, Singapore, Toronto or Vancouver, Amsterdam, or Sydney. If you are not selected in the lottery, transfer abroad for at least 12 months, then return on an L-1. Time spent in the U.S. does not count toward the year.
- **Other points:** there is no cap, and the H-1B fees do not apply. L-1B "specialized knowledge" petitions have historically drawn many requests for evidence. While abroad, you can also stay in the H-1B lottery, but that route means consular processing.

### 4.4 Day-1 CPT: a candid assessment
- **What it is:** enrolling in another program, usually a second master's, that authorizes CPT from the first day, mainly so you can keep working after OPT runs out or the lottery fails.
- **2026 status [IN EFFECT]:**
  - SEVP's Aug 12 and Aug 24, 2026 broadcasts say CPT must be a **required** part of the curriculum for **all** students, and degree completion must depend on it.
  - Credit or enrollment alone does not qualify, and electives do not qualify.
  - The broadcasts warn DSOs that they are liable for improper CPT authorizations [14][15][68].
  - This guidance **stands despite the D/S injunction** [67].
- **Risks [ASSESSMENT + background]:**
  - SEVIS termination and loss of status if the CPT is found improper.
  - Findings of past status violations when you later apply for an H-1B change of status, an extension, or a green card.
  - The school could lose its SEVP certification.
  - Extra visa scrutiny when you travel.
  - Separately, the Aug 2025 D/S proposal would have limited moves to a same-level or lower degree program. Whether that survived into the final rule was not verified, and the final rule is enjoined anyway.
- **Bottom line:** **do not plan on Day-1 CPT as a fallback in 2026-2027.** If you are considering a specific program, get an **independent** immigration attorney to review it; do not rely on the school's admissions or marketing staff.

### 4.5 Other routes **[background]**
- **A real PhD or other higher-degree program** gives you new OPT and possibly a second STEM extension.
- **Employer-sponsored green card (PERM, then I-140):** people born in mainland China face **multi-year EB-2 and EB-3 backlogs**; check the State Department's monthly Visa Bulletin. You still need H-1B or another status in the meantime.
- **EB-2 National Interest Waiver or EB-1A self-petition:** possible for strong research profiles, but the same China backlog applies to EB-2.
- **H-1B1, E-3, TN:** not available to PRC nationals.

### 4.6 How finance and fintech employers' willingness to sponsor changed in 2025-2026
**Caveat:** I found **no survey or official data specific to finance or fintech**. Most of this subsection is **[ASSESSMENT]** built on the general evidence below.

**Evidence:**
- FY2027 registrations fell about 38% overall, and outsourcing firms' registrations fell 92% [3][40][46].
- Only about 700 people had the $100K paid for them, which suggests employers essentially stopped sponsoring people from abroad but kept filing changes of status inside the U.S. [39].

**Assessment:**
- The weighted lottery rewards high-paying employers. Quant trading firms, hedge funds, and large banks, which usually pay new grads well above Level II, are **relatively advantaged**.
- The **proposed $103,265 fee is the swing factor.** If it is finalized, expect many mid-size fintechs, startups, insurers, mortgage and lending companies, and non-tech finance employers to stop registering new grads. Elite quant firms and large banks may keep sponsoring select hires.
- **Employer-level signal:** aggregator LCA data (h1bdata.info, H1BGrader, MyVisaJobs) gathered in this project's other employer research shows many OC/LA finance and fintech employers with LCAs through FY2025. At least one, Hyundai Capital America, showed **zero FY2026 LCAs** on H1BGrader. Aggregator data lags and is incomplete, so treat this as a reason to **ask directly**, not as proof.

## 5. If you are a PRC national **[background, not re-verified in this research; confirm nationality first]**
- **Proclamation 10043 (May 2020)** bars F/J entry for certain PRC graduate students and researchers tied to entities supporting China's "military-civil fusion" strategy. It can matter when you renew an F-1 visa to re-enter. Check whether it applies to your undergraduate institution.
- **Visa renewals and travel:** PRC nationals in STEM fields often face extra security review (221(g)) that can take weeks or months. Avoid international travel during critical windows: OPT unemployment, a pending change of status, or a pending STEM extension.
- **2025 State Department statements:** in 2025 the State Department announced tougher visa scrutiny of some Chinese students (CCP ties or critical fields). Later statements softened. Treat this as a travel-risk factor.
- **Export controls:** semiconductor, advanced-computing, AI-hardware, aerospace, and defense employers may need a Commerce Department **"deemed export" license** before a PRC national can access controlled technology. **ITAR and security-clearance roles** generally require U.S. persons. Filter these out or ask about them early.
- **Travel-ban lists:** China was not on the 2025 lists, as far as I know; confirm.

## 6. What this means for the job search

### 6.1 Employer checks, in priority order
1. **E-Verify enrollment of the exact hiring entity.** This is required for STEM OPT. Check E-Verify's public employer search [74] or ask HR. Also confirm:
   - they will **sign Form I-983**;
   - you will be their **W-2 employee**, not a 1099 contractor or a staffing-vendor placement.
2. **Recent H-1B cap filings for new grads.** Check the USCIS H-1B Employer Data Hub (initial "new employment" approvals) and DOL LCA disclosure data [74]. **Give the most weight to FY2025-FY2026**, because policies changed during 2025-2026.
3. **Wage level of the offer.** Look up the likely occupation code and worksite area on FLAG [74] and compare with the base salary. Aim for **Level II or higher**, which doubles your lottery weight over Level I. Remember the DOL proposal could raise the thresholds.
4. **Cap-exempt status:** UC and other universities, affiliated nonprofits, research organizations.
5. **Offices outside the U.S.**, which make an L-1 fallback possible, and any history of O-1 or L-1 use.
6. **Recent layoffs in similar roles.** Under the Sep 18, 2026 order, such employers may face more scrutiny.
7. **Export-control or U.S.-person requirements** (if you are a PRC national).
8. **Avoid "OPT placement" or consulting firms** that place workers at client sites. They create STEM OPT training-plan problems, and the pending H-1B reform proposal targets third-party placement [75].

### 6.2 How to ask about sponsorship
- **Raise it at the first recruiter screen**, not after the offer. Put it positively: you need **no sponsorship to start**, and you have **up to 3 years** of work authorization.
- Sample questions:
  - *"I'm on F-1 OPT and, as a STEM graduate, eligible for up to three years of work authorization in total. The STEM extension only requires that the employer be enrolled in E-Verify and sign a Form I-983 training plan. Is [Company] enrolled in E-Verify?"*
  - *"Does [Company] register new-grad hires in the H-1B lottery? Did you register anyone in March 2026? Would the proposed H-1B cap fee change that policy if it's finalized?"*
  - *"If I'm not selected, are there alternatives, such as a transfer to an office outside the U.S. or O-1 sponsorship?"*
  - *"When does the company usually start the green-card (PERM) process?"*
- **Application forms:** answer "Will you now or in the future require sponsorship?" **truthfully**. The honest answer is "future: yes," for the H-1B. Misrepresenting this risks a rescinded offer and legal problems.
- **Get commitments in writing where you can**, for example in the offer letter or an email. No employer can guarantee a lottery result.

### 6.3 Timeline and pitfalls
**Scenario A: your program ended around June 2026 (spring quarter)**
- The OPT filing deadline was **60 days after your program end date**, around August 2026. **If you did not file, you have no post-completion OPT for this degree**, because it cannot be filed late. See the International Center immediately.
- If your EAD is active, your **90-day unemployment count started on the EAD start date**, not the date you got a job offer. **Any** qualifying job stops the count: 20 or more hours a week and directly related to your degree. Unpaid volunteer work counts during regular OPT but not STEM OPT. **Report the job within 10 days.**
- Make your **first job one with an E-Verify employer**, so you can move straight into STEM OPT. File the STEM extension up to 90 days before your EAD expires.

**Scenario B: your program ends around December 2026 (fall quarter)**
- Your filing window **opens 90 days before the program end date, which is about now**, and closes 60 days after it. File soon after your DSO's recommendation, and in any case within 30 days of it. Consider premium processing if you have a fixed start date.
- Your requested start date must fall within the 60-day window after graduation. The 90-day unemployment count starts on the EAD start date, so do not choose an earlier start date than you need.
- If the D/S rule is revived, filing before its transition date could spare you the extra I-539. Watch the litigation.

**Either scenario**
- **Lottery attempts:** with 36 months of OPT plus STEM OPT, you get about **3 tries**: March 2027 (FY2028), March 2028 (FY2029), and March 2029 (FY2030). An employer has to have you hired by about February to register you, and the rules may differ each year (fee and wage proposals).
- **Cap-gap:** if you are selected and the change-of-status petition is filed on time, your status and work authorization continue through **April 1** (background).
- **Do not travel abroad while an H-1B change of status is pending.** Travel abandons it and forces consular processing, where the $100K fee would apply if reinstated, and where visa delays are likely.
- **Keep your status clean.** Going over the 90- or 150-day unemployment limit, or working without authorization, can lead to a denied change of status and then consular processing.
- **Changing employers during STEM OPT:** the new employer must be in E-Verify, you need a **new I-983 within 10 days**, and you must report the change.
- **Keep records:** your I-983s, offer letters, and pay stubs.
